# My top 5 Novels of all time

Spoiler Alert: This list is going to be dominated by Fantasy books because that's the genre I tend to read the most. So in no particular order, my top 5 book of all time.

---
1) **A Memory of Light** (Robert Jordan and Brandon Sanderson): One of the biggest struggles that many authors and screenwriters face, is writing a satisfying conclusion to an elaborate story that they have been building up for years. Giving the fans a satisfying ending, is even more difficult in Epic-Fantasy stories due to the added layers of depth and complexity in the worldbuilding itself. Brandon Sanderson, somehow managed to defy all odds and gifted Wheel of Time fans with one of the most satisfying conclusions to a series ever, despite only having the original author's notes as a reference. 

_A Memory of Light_ has some of my favourtie moments in the entire series, from Demandread's arrival at the Last Battle, to the final confrontation between Rand and Shai'tan, with a lot more sprinkled in between. The Demandread reveal was one of the best in the series, and the chapter _Those who Fight_ highlighted the growth that each one of the characters had undergone over the past 14 books. In my opinion, this single chapter makes the meandering in books 8 and 9 worth it in the end. We look back upon the journey fondly, yet slowly realise it's coming to an end. 

It manages to tie up multiple plotlines and give us a satsfying ending. There are neither beginings nor endings to the Wheel of Time, but this is an ending. 

(Note: I would rather get waterboarded than read the Androl POV chapters from this book again)

---
2) **A Feast For Crows** (George R.R Martin): Most _A Song of Ice and Fire_ fans would probably consider _Storm of Swords_ to be the strongest book in the series, with the best plot progression and most engaging storylines. While, I do feel the same, _Feast_ contains some of Martin's best character work, while simultaneously having satisfying worldbuilding. 

Jaime Lannister is one of my all time favourite ficitonal characters, and it is because of this book. This book focuses on Jaime's identity struggle after losing his hand, who can no longer claim to be one of the most formidable sword masters in Westeros. There is also a focus his relationship with Cersie, and how he slowly manages to break free from her influence. Jaime is trying to be a better man, but his reputation as a Kingslayer only gets him scorn.

Brienne's storyline provides a breath of fresh air, as we finally get to witness the struggles of Westerosi commoners. Most of the plotlines up until this point have been on the political struggles between the Great Houses of Westeros, and the Brienne POV chapters in this book finally gives us an idea of the repurcussions of the wars waged on the average person. 

I'm not even going to get into how the show absolutely massacred the Dorne and Iron Islands plotlines. Every single Euron chapter had me at the edge of my seat, and the _Fire and Blood_ reveal (possibly foreshadowing another Blackfyre rebellion?) in the final Dorne chapter was absolutely insane.
